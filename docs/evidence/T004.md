## Findings

- French A1 follows the FEI A1 inventory for present forms, simple statements/questions and negation, noun phrases, agreement, quantity, prepositions and short links. The scope keeps verb families and imperatives to frequent patterns.
- German A1 uses the official Start Deutsch 1 grammar inventory. A borderline point is resolved explicitly: polite `möchte` / `würde gern` are A1 memorised request patterns; the general Konjunktiv II paradigm is not an A1 skill. The cited Goethe A1 document distinguishes lexical courtesy forms from systematic grammar treatment.
- German A2 expands the case system, past forms, clause order, common subordinate clauses, comparisons and pronouns against the Fit in Deutsch 2 inventory. Its detailed source is a youth specification; the current adult exam overview confirms everyday A2 functions but does not settle each grammar boundary. `de-a2-020` (the scope of `zu` + infinitive) stays provisional pending an adult-level language specialist/reference review.
- English A1 has broad topic coverage from Cambridge A1 Movers, but the source is designed for young learners. Adult transfer is a local design inference, recorded as `local-alignment` where relevant. An adult A1 grammar reference would strengthen this track.
- English A2 follows the Cambridge A2 Key language specifications. The inventory scopes present perfect, passive, reported clauses, conditionals, relative clauses and other broad categories to simple familiar uses. Cambridge notes learners may encounter forms beyond those directly tested; this is not a claim that every listed form is a universal A2 requirement.
- The A1/A2 boundary is not prescribed as a fixed grammar syllabus by the CEFR. The inventory makes a local, source-backed progression; the Council of Europe describes the CEFR as a non-prescriptive framework and notes that it does not validate specific exam-to-CEFR claims. The CSV therefore uses source alignment language and makes no certification claim.
- The A2 grammar skills are not copied from Kwiziq or any other commercial grammar catalogue. No Kwiziq material, exercises, assets or implementation were used.
